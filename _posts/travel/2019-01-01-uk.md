### Reasons to go:

##### 1. [London](/travel/london/)



##### <a name="edinburgh"></a> 2. Edinburgh
* Best views of the city: Calton Hill, Arthur's Seat in Holyrood Park (don't miss the Scottish Parliament Building nearby).
* Old Town: St Giles' Cathedral, Victoria Terrace, walking the narrow streets.
* New Town: elegant architecture of Moray Place, Circus Lane, etc.
* Castles: Edinburgh Castle, Craigmillar Castle (my favourite).
* Art galleries: National Galleries of Scotland, National Galleries of Scotland Modern One and Modern Two, Edinburgh Arts & Picture Framers on Nicolson Street (great Scotland themed postcards).
* Museums: Surgeons' Hall Museum, National Museum of Scotland.
* Hiking: Pentland Hills Regional Park.
* Other stuff: The Meadows (park), the walk along Water of Leith (past Dean Village), Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh.
* Stockbridge neighbourhood is the best place to stay.
* Restaurants: The Outsider Restaurant, The Lookout by Gardener's Cottage. On my wishlist: Purslane Restaurant, The Cottage at Royal Terrace.
* Cocktails: Hey Palu, The Last Word, Panda & Sons, Bramble, Lucky Liquor Co, Nightcap, Nauticus. The Scotch Malt Whisky Society for whisky.
* On my wishlist: boat tours from [Scottish Seabird Centre](https://www.seabird.org/boats) to see puffins.



##### 3. Hiking

**3.1 Lake District National Park**
* Striding Edge hike - ridge walk to/from Helvellyn. Start from Patterdale or the bus stop/car park at (54.542464, -3.058717).
* Other hikes: Crinkle Crags, Stickle tarn, Scafell Pike, Hardknott Roman Fort, Buttermere (Buttermere village, Red Pike, along the ridge to High Stile, return along the north-east side of Buttermere lake).
* Note: a great way to visit without a car is to take the train to Windermere, stay there and take [buses](https://lakedistrictonboard.com/transport/lake-district-buses/#stagecoach) to starting points of different hikes. Usually a pdf of current bus schedule can be found.

**3.2 Brecon Beacons National Park**
* Llyn y Fan Fach circular hike is my favourite of the two hikes I did. Start from the free parking at (51.899802, -3.745603). Cross the bridge at (51.899485, -3.744223) and head up to Waun Lefrith (51.878681, -3.747141). On the way there check if you'll be able to cross Afon Sawdde river at (51.890747, -3.739819) on your way back. If not, check if you'll be able to cross at (51.883836, -3.740053). From Waun Lefrith (51.878681, -3.747141), continue along the ridge to Picws Du (51.882617, -3.727827). If it hasn't rained for a while, it is enjoyable to continue directly to Fan Brycheiniog (51.882211, -3.708217), otherwise the best route is via Fan Foel (51.88713, -3.714687). Next head down to Llyn y Fan Fawr lake (51.878487, -3.697168) where you can swim (just avoid the leeches). Walk around the lake and enjoy some of the best views from (51.884627, -3.704198) and (51.888798, -3.718685). Head back to the parking via an Afon Sawdde river crossing you've picked earlier, or if neither of the two seemed appealing try the route that goes via (51.9053, -3.733062). [My hike](https://connect.garmin.com/app/activity/23332845150) was 13.5km with 670m total ascent.
* Pen-y-Fan Horseshoe circular hike is very similar, and much more popular. Doing both hikes makes for an enjoyable weekend; but if you are not looking to fill a weekend, there is no need to do both. Start from the free parking at (51.843705, -3.399637), or the free overflow parking at (51.838082, -3.391301). Cross the stream at (51.853529, -3.403939), cross the bridge at (51.852124, -3.409132), head up to (51.849611, -3.421684), and continue to Cefn Cul (51.863349, -3.432719). Climb the three peaks - Corn Du (51.881683, -3.443655), Pen y Fan (51.884041, -3.437008) and Cribyn (51.881794, -3.419635). Then head down to the saddle point at (51.874924, -3.408075), from there you can head back to the parking via (51.867366, -3.404173) or make it into a bigger loop. The bit between (51.878446, -3.445299) and Pen y Fan is very busy, as most people park at (51.86872, -3.472004) to get a selfie on the famous Pen y Fan. [My hike](https://connect.garmin.com/app/activity/23321140360) was 15.5km with 690m total ascent.
* If you happen to be in Cardiff, best coffee is at Mec Coffee, Sway, and Sibling. Pettigrew Bakeries has good sourdough. See the juxtaposition of Senedd and Norwegian Church Arts Centre. Pontcanna (think Kings Road and Severn Grove) is the best area to stay.

**3.3 Other stuff:**
* [Whitelee Windfarm](https://www.whiteleewindfarm.co.uk/outdoor-pursuits) - walking among wind turbines and sheep. 



##### 4. Oxford
* Extremely pretty architecture: all the colleges, Bodleian Library, Sheldonian Theatre.
* Museums: Oxford University Museum of Natural History, Pitt Rivers Museum, Ashmolean Museum. All of them are free, no bookings required.
* Coffee: New Ground Coffee Oxford, Society Café.



##### 5. Bristol and around

Southville:
* [Sweven Coffee](https://www.swevencoffee.co.uk/) - my favourite coffee place in Bristol.
* Graffiti hunting - a lot of it is along North street, Luckwell road, West street and East street. See [UpFest map](https://www.upfest.co.uk/theme/Live/upfest-map.pdf) or this [Google My Maps](https://inspiringcity.com/2024/08/25/upfest-presents-2024-all-the-street-art-and-murals/). Upfest Gallery itself is on my wishlist. The street art festival happens every 2 years, so there is a steady supply of new murals.
* On my wishlist: COR Restaurant. Albatross Café might be nice.

Spike Island:
* [Root](https://www.rootbristol.co.uk/) - my favourite restaurant in Bristol. From here you also get great views of cranes outside M Shed museum and the surrounding area.
* My favourite view of Bristol's colourful houses is from Bristol Marina (51.448102, -2.610924).
* Underfall Yard is fun. Visit the Engine House (51.44685, -2.618236); engine demos run on weekend mornings. 
* A tiny ferry runs between SS Great Britain Ferry Landing and Hannover Quay. The journey is fast and cheap. And you get to catch a glimpse of SS Great Britain ship.
* On my wishlist: Spike Island art gallery (free).

Central Bristol:
* Walk up to Cabot Tower for great views. Climbing to the top of the tower (free) is on my wishlist.
* Wills Memorial Building is beautiful on the outside. I believe the inside is magnificent too, taking a [tour](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/university/visit/tower-tours/) is on my wishlist.
* On my wishlist: Bristol Cathedral, [The Mount Without](https://www.themountwithout.co.uk/) (an intriguing venue, not sure if easy to visit), Arnolfini Arts (free art gallery), tasting menu at [Wilson's Restaurant](https://www.wilsonsbristol.co.uk/). Millennium Square in the evening might be fun.
* Graffiti hunting along Stokes Croft. This street, that changes its name from Stokes Croft to Cheltenham road to Gloucester road, holds the title of UK's longest independent shopping street. If you get hungry along the way, I'd try one of the Sri Lankan restaurants - Nadu or The Coconut Tree.

Old Town:
* The area around Broad street and Corn street has a lot of beautiful old buildings. [This pdf](https://bristololdcity.co.uk/sites/default/files/old-city-heritage-trail-map.pdf) makes for a fine self-guided tour.
* Full Court Press is a nice coffee place. Urban Tandoor is a nice Indian restaurant.
* On my wishlist: tasting menu at [Adelina Yard](https://www.adelinayard.com/).

Clifton:
* Clifton Suspension Bridge. A [hard hat tour](https://cliftonbridge.org.uk/whats-happening/) is on my wishlist. There are also other tours.
* On my wishlist: exploring Clifton Village and a long walk through Leigh Woods.

**Near Bristol:**
* Just outside central Bristol: on my wishlist are Aerospace Bristol museum, University of Bristol Botanic Garden and Arnos Vale Cemetery.
* Bath: The Roman Baths. City's unique architecture, good examples include The Circus and The Royal Crescents. Bath Abbey decorated with carvings of [angels climbing the ladders](https://curiousrambler.com/the-upside-down-angels-of-bath-abbey/). On my wishlist: Museum of Bath Architecture.
* Stonehenge is exciting. Avebury is on my wishlist.



##### 6. Birmingham and around
* The Jewellery Quarter - a lovely neighbourhood. 
* Street art in Digbeth: Custard Factory, [The Brolly Works public parking](https://goo.gl/maps/bd1hN4YEQARatiHc9), etc. I used this [map](https://visitbirmingham.com/things-to-see-and-do/graffiti-art-of-digbeth-walk-p1346341) as a rough guide.
* Lapworth Museum of Geology - a paradise of rocks and fossils. Fluorescent rocks on the upper floor are magnificent.
* Gas Street Basin - an area of canals. Ikon Gallery is great.
* Restaurants: Land (vegan tasting menu), Chakana (Peruvian restaurant with vegan options).
* Cocktails: Fox and Chance, Couch Stirchley.

**Near Birmingham:**
* Lichfield cathedral is beautiful. The rooftop tour is still on my wishlist.
* Coventry Transport Museum is excellent, with [Thrust SuperSonic Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ThrustSSC) on display.



### Notes:
* Trainline app is the best tool for planning train journeys, and tracking trains in real time.
* National Parks in the UK do not have entrance fees or opening hours.
